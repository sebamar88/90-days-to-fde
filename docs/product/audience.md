# Audience and learner outcomes

## Primary learner

An experienced software engineer, typically comfortable shipping and maintaining production software, who wants to move toward Forward Deployed Engineering or Applied AI Engineering. They can code independently but may need practice with discovery, customer communication, AI system evaluation, enterprise constraints, and delivery under ambiguity.

## Not designed for

- People learning programming fundamentals.
- Learners seeking a prompt cookbook or a vendor certification study guide.
- Anyone expecting a guaranteed role, fixed daily workload, or one-click project portfolio.

## By the end, a learner should be able to

- Interview stakeholders and distinguish stated requests from underlying workflow problems.
- Define measurable outcomes, constraints, assumptions, and a small useful scope.
- Design an AI-enabled system with explicit boundaries, data flow, fallbacks, and human review where needed.
- Build and evaluate a solution with representative cases, including failure cases.
- Communicate reliability, security, privacy, latency, and cost trade-offs.
- Plan a deployment, support model, adoption path, and customer handoff.
- Present evidence of decisions and outcomes in a portfolio without disclosing confidential data.

## Accessibility and pacing

The day number denotes sequence, not a prescribed number of hours. Learners may pause, revisit, or combine days. Lessons should avoid time-sensitive provider features where possible, include text alternatives for diagrams, use readable language, and distinguish required core work from optional extensions.
