# Day 5 — Run the discovery conversation

## Why this matters

Discovery is a listening and sense-making exercise. In a simulation, you can practice following evidence rather than steering toward your preferred architecture.

## Scenario

Role-play a 30-minute interview. One person is the coordinator; the other is the engineer. If working alone, use the simulation card below and answer as the participant would based on the stated facts, marking invented detail as simulated.

## Simulation card

The coordinator says: “Some requests take longer because the email leaves out a building or unit. We search old messages, call the requester, and sometimes the technician sends it back.” Do not infer frequency, cost, or severity unless asked; the participant has not measured them.

## Outcomes

- Use follow-up questions to reconstruct a recent case.
- Notice when an answer is a generalization rather than a specific example.
- Avoid collecting personal or confidential data.

## Exercise

Conduct or write a simulated interview using Day 4's guide. Ask what happened, who acted, what information was missing, how it was resolved, and what impact followed. Capture direct quotes separately from your interpretation. Mark any unsupplied answer `[SIMULATED]`.

## Deliverable

`interview-notes.md` with at least one event timeline and a list of unanswered questions.

## Assessment

Pass if the notes preserve the distinction between supplied facts and simulated details, contain a concrete sequence, and do not turn “sometimes” into a numeric claim.

## Reflection

Where did you feel tempted to suggest a feature instead of asking another question?
